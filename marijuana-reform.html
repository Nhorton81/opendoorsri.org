Marijuana Reform

Rhode Island has become the 15th state in the country to decriminalize the possession of small amounts of marijuana.  The legislation was sponsored by Representative Edwards of Tiverton and Senator Josh Miller of Cranston, and Governor Chafee signed the law on June 13.  

Rhode Island decriminalized the possession of small amounts of marijuana. The legislation was sponsored by Representative Edwards of Tiverton and Senator Josh Miller of Cranston, and Governor Chafee signed the law on June 13. Starting April 1, 2013, the possession of one ounce of marijuana or less will be a civil offense, like a traffic ticket. Instead of being charged with a crime, it will result in a ticket of $150. Juveniles will also be required to go to drug awareness counseling. A third offense within 18 months will result in a misdemeanor charge.

The Criminal Justice Costs of Marijuana Prohibition 

Released: March 2010 (pdf)

Rhode Island criminalized the possession of small amounts of marijuana.  This policy has widespread and significant fiscal and human costs, results in unfair and racially biased punishment, and has no demonstrated public safety benefits. 

Open the PDF

In 2009, there were 1,771 arrests for first-time marijuana possession in Rhode Island. In 2008, there were 584 incidents of incarceration for marijuana possession. Ending prohibition and eliminating these arrests would save the state approximately $12.7 million in criminal justice costs.
